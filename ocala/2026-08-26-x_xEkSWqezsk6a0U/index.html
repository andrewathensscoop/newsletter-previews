Subject: 🍎 Marion board approves nearly $6M teacher contract

Preview text: Inside: two data-center meetings, Big Lee's brisket, Rock The Country, and a $1.5M Ocala home.

In today's Digest:

  • 🍎 Marion's school board approved nearly $6 million in teacher salary and benefit increases

  • 🌳 Falling trees damaged two Ocala homes during Saturday's storms
  • 🤝 $1.7 million will fund nine United Way programs in Marion County
  • 🎨 Fourteen art spaces are relaunching Ocala's monthly studio and gallery tour

🔥 Ocala Pitmaster's Brisket and Burnt Ends

At 2611 SW 19th Avenue Road, Big Lee's Serious About BBQ is pitmaster Rashad Jones's brick-and-mortar home for handcrafted, small-batch barbecue after years on the road with food trucks.

Brisket, burnt ends, ribs, pulled pork, chicken, and sausage fill the trays. Jones won Food Network's BBQ Brawl and earned the show's Master of 'Cue title, giving Ocala a hometown smokehouse with a nationally recognized pitmaster.

🍎 Board Approves Nearly $6M Teacher Contract

The Gist: The Marion County School Board unanimously approved a revised contract with the Marion Education Association that adds $5.9 million in salaries and benefits. Union members ratified the tentative agreement last week, and the revision is retroactive to July 1.

Why It Matters: The deal changes teacher compensation early in the school year and commits nearly $6 million in district resources.

Read more here.

🖥️ Data-Center Debate Heads to Two County Meetings

The Gist: An Aug. 24 community workshop drew residents worried about data centers' water and power demand and others who see jobs and economic opportunity. Attendees urged county leaders to set clear rules before approving future sites.

Why It Matters: Those rules could shape how Marion balances development with Silver Springs, water use, power infrastructure, and nearby neighborhoods.

What's Next: The Planning and Zoning Commission will consider a temporary moratorium Aug. 31 at 5:30 p.m. County commissioners hold a data-center workshop Sept. 1 at 2 p.m. at the McPherson campus.

Read more here.

🌳 Storms Send Trees Through Two Ocala Homes

The Gist: Saturday storms toppled trees onto two Ocala homes. In the 3100 block of SE Eighth Street, a tree cut through the roof and damaged the kitchen, living room, and a bedroom; another hit a fence and roof in the 3700 block of East Fort King Street. No injuries were reported.

Why It Matters: The damage shows how quickly summer storms can turn falling trees and power lines into neighborhood hazards.

Read more here.

🤝 $1.7M Backs Nine United Way Programs

The Gist: United Way of Marion County marked 65 years at a College of Central Florida gathering. The nonprofit raised nearly $2 million last year, is directing $1.7 million to nine programs, and highlighted an estimated 15,000 Marion County children living in poverty.

Why It Matters: The money supports youth opportunity, financial security, healthy communities, and stronger families as the county keeps growing.

Read more here.

🎨 Fourteen Art Stops Relaunch Monthly Studio Tour

The Gist: Fourteen Ocala studios and galleries are relaunching the Ocala Studio & Gallery Tour with a free preview at Shapot Art Studio & Gallery, 2318 NE Eighth Road. Visitors can meet artists, see work, and learn about classes before monthly tours begin.

Why It Matters: The program links major institutions such as the Appleton Museum and Webber Gallery with smaller studios, giving local artists a regular way to reach the public.

What's Next: The preview runs Sept. 19 from 6 to 9 p.m. Monthly first-Saturday tours begin Oct. 3 from 11 a.m. to 3 p.m.
