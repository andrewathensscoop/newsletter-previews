🔥 Pollo a la Brasa and Ceviche on Highway 484

At 2105 SW Highway 484, Mi Tierra Latina is a family-owned Ocala restaurant where Peruvian and Mexican cooking share one kitchen, a combination the family has served locally since 2013.

Start with the charcoal-roasted pollo a la brasa or citrusy ceviche. Fajitas, house-made guacamole, arroz chaufa, anticuchos, and plantains give the Mexican and Peruvian sides of the menu equal room.

💸 Vendor Email Scam Costs Ocala $277,000

The Gist: Ocala officials confirmed at the Aug. 4 City Council meeting that the city lost $277,000 after an email appeared to come from a legitimate vendor. Council member James Hilty said the case dates to May, while City Manager Peter Lee said the investigation remains active and disclosing details could hinder recovery.

Why It Matters: The city is trying to recover a substantial loss of public money while residents await details on how the payment cleared internal controls.

What's Next: The investigation is continuing, and officials have not announced a recovery timeline.

🏫 South Marion High Opens With 2,011 Seats

The Gist: Marion County Public Schools opened the 2026-27 year Aug. 10 with a projected 46,697 students across 54 traditional and five charter schools. New South Marion High near Marion Oaks opened with capacity for 2,011 students, while the district added more than 120 teachers and still has 62 vacancies.

Why It Matters: The county's first new high school in 26 years adds space in fast-growing southwest Marion and will expand to 2,711 seats.

What's Next: All pre-K and kindergarten students report together Aug. 13, and the district's new mobile app is scheduled for Sept. 1.

📚 Wyomina Park Rezoning Put on Hold

The Gist: The Marion County School Board voted Aug. 11 to postpone rezoning Wyomina Park Elementary under its Future Focused Operations Plan. Parents and teachers opposed moving students from the county's only school participating in a year-round pilot program.

Why It Matters: The delay keeps students in place for now while the district weighs a broader consolidation plan for rural northern Marion County.

What's Next: The district plans community workshops in early September before returning to the proposal.

Read more here.

🦠 Marion Records Second Vibrio Case and a Death

The Gist: The Florida Department of Health reported that a Marion County resident died after contracting Vibrio vulnificus. It was the county's second case this year; Florida had logged 14 cases and two deaths.

Why It Matters: The report does not identify where the person was exposed, but the case is a timely warning about open wounds in warm salt or brackish water and eating raw shellfish.

Read more here.

🐊 Silver River Reopens After Alligator Attack

The Gist: A woman swimming near Ray Wayside Park was hospitalized Aug. 8 after an alligator bit her arm. The Silver River closed for about three hours, and FWC officers and a nuisance trapper removed a 9-foot-8-inch alligator before the river reopened.

Why It Matters: The incident is the fourth reported across Central and North Central Florida this summer; FWC advises swimming only in designated areas during daylight.
