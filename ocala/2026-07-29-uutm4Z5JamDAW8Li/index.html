🏫 Marion School Employees to Pay About $500 More

The Gist: The Marion County School Board voted 3-2 on July 28 to reduce the district's contribution to its self-funded health plan. Employees will pay about $500 more per year through higher premiums and deductibles, and Board Chair Sarah James said the alternative was cutting jobs.

Why It Matters: The decision shifts part of the district's budget pressure directly onto school employees.

Read more here.

🚰 Boil-Water Notice Covers Pine Run Area

The Gist: A July 27 water main break placed Marion County utility customers in the Pine Run community and along Southwest 105th Street under a boil-water notice. Crews completed a valve installation after the break.

Why It Matters: Affected households need to boil tap water before drinking or cooking until the county confirms it is safe.

What's Next: The notice remains in effect until water-quality testing comes back clear.

Read more here.

🧬 MCSO First in U.S. to Receive Rapid DNA Result

The Gist: The Marion County Sheriff's Office became the first law-enforcement agency in the country to receive a result through a rapid DNA system. The new MCSO and FDLE program is designed to speed up sample processing, which typically takes 170 to 466 days depending on the case.

Why It Matters: Shorter lab delays could help local investigators connect cases and identify known offenders much faster.

Read more here.

⚖️ Dunnellon Fire Lawsuit Faces Venue Fight

The Gist: G Law Group, representing victims of the Dunnellon railroad-tie fire, filed a July 10 motion seeking to return the case to Florida state court. CSX Transportation and Florida Northern Railroad moved the lawsuit to federal court in June.

Why It Matters: The venue decision will shape how a local community's claims over a creosote-fueled fire proceed against two railroad companies.

What's Next: The court has not yet ruled on whether the lawsuit will stay in federal court or return to state court.

Read more here.

🏛️ Ocala Moves Toward Citywide Kratom Ban

The Gist: Mayor Ben Marciano and Ocala City Council members agreed July 21 to develop an ordinance banning kratom sales within city limits. City leaders cited reported medical emergencies and suspected overdoses involving the legally sold herbal product.

Why It Matters: A citywide ban would remove kratom from Ocala smoke-shop shelves and set a local rule while state action remains uncertain.

What's Next: City staff will develop the proposed ordinance for a future council vote.
