🦐 Fried seafood at the end of Shem Creek

The Wreck of the Richard and Charlene

At 106 Haddrell Street near the end of Shem Creek, The Wreck of the Richard and Charlene is a first-come, first-served seafood shack serving shrimp, scallops, oysters, and the day's catch fried, grilled, or boiled.

The name comes from a trawler Hurricane Hugo drove across the dock in 1989. Paper-plated seafood, red rice, hush puppies, and shrimp boats outside keep the room tied to the working creek around it.

🏘️ Hanahan Town Center Goes Local-Only

🏘️ Hanahan Town Center Goes Local-Only

The Gist: Blackwell's Corner will bring locally owned restaurants, shops, offices and a central lawn to the former Blackwell's Hardware site in Hanahan. The first tenants include Untitled Pizza Co., Kudu Coffee & Wine, Pineapple House Boutique and a new Italian market from Cane Pazzo owner Mark Bolchoz.

Why It Matters: The project replaces a hardware store that operated from 1947 to 2025 while giving Hanahan a walkable gathering place without national chains.

What's Next: A temporary Blackwell's Outpost opens in September, groundbreaking follows that month and the full town center is slated for fall 2027.

🔋 IEB Brings First U.S. Plant to North Charleston

🔋 IEB Brings First U.S. Plant to North Charleston

The Gist: IEB Americas, a subsidiary of German battery-charging and power-supply maker Industrie Elektronik Brilon, is investing $1.5 million in its first U.S. production facility. The Carolina Commerce Parkway operation will handle manufacturing, warehousing, service and training and create 30 jobs.

Why It Matters: The facility adds advanced-manufacturing jobs and puts the company's North American production and customer support in North Charleston.

What's Next: The company is directing job seekers to its LinkedIn page for openings.

🚁 Folly Beach Reworks Its Drone Rules

🚁 Folly Beach Reworks Its Drone Rules

The Gist: Folly Beach is considering a revised ordinance that would require drone operators on the beach to carry certifications and permits, with Beach Patrol handling enforcement. The city broadened an earlier drone-fishing proposal after SCDNR warned it could conflict with state rules.

Why It Matters: Officials are responding to bait dropped near swimmers, large fish pulled through swimming areas and fishing lines that can entangle surfers.

What's Next: If advanced, the proposal must return for second reading. Council has not set possible penalties.

🏫 Two New Berkeley Schools Open

🏫 Two New Berkeley Schools Open

The Gist: Nearly 40,000 Berkeley County students return Wednesday, including about 1,300 at the new Bee Tree Elementary and 1,100 at the new Midtown Middle. A renovated Hanahan Middle also opens, making the three campuses the first completed projects from the voter-approved 2022 penny sales tax.

Why It Matters: Berkeley County has added roughly 400 students a year for nearly a decade, forcing new boundaries that affect more than 10 schools.

What's Next: The district begins the year with 18 teacher vacancies, down from 73 in late June, while penny-tax work continues at three high schools.

🏛️ Former YWCA Demolished for CofC Dorm Site

🏛️ Former YWCA Demolished for CofC Dorm Site

The Gist: The College of Charleston demolished the 5,000-square-foot former YWCA headquarters at 106 Coming Street for a proposed dormitory. The YWCA occupied the building beginning in 1964, and the property covers part of an 18th-century paupers' cemetery where thousands are believed to have been buried.

Why It Matters: The removal erased a place tied to Charleston's Black institutional history while reviving concerns about how the college communicates work around possible graves.

What's Next: CofC still needs state approvals to remove the surrounding asphalt and has proposed a mural and plaque commemorating the YWCA.
