🇵🇭 Filipino family-style plates on Rutledge Avenue

Kultura

At 267 Rutledge Avenue, Kultura is chef Nikko Cagalanan and Paula Kramer’s family-style Filipino restaurant, inspired by Cagalanan’s grandmother, Lola Mansueta. The couple moved it into a larger home in the same neighborhood in October 2025.

Kamayan Sundays trade plates and utensils for a banana-leaf spread meant to be shared by hand. The regular dinner menu pairs familiar Filipino flavors with local ingredients, and Cagalanan was a 2024 James Beard Emerging Chef finalist.

🏫 School Tax Extension Heads to Voters

🏫 School Tax Extension Heads to Voters

The Gist: The Charleston County School District board voted Monday to put an extension of the existing 1-cent education sales tax on the November ballot. The measure would keep the rate unchanged and fund 23 projects through 2034, including eight replacement schools, a new West Ashley elementary school, a districtwide Montessori middle school and more pre-K capacity.

Why It Matters: The vote puts countywide school construction in residents' hands, while district officials say visitors generate roughly 60% of the program's revenue.

What's Next: Charleston County voters will decide whether to extend the tax on the November ballot.

🚁 IOP Approves First-Responder Drone

🚁 IOP Approves First-Responder Drone

The Gist: Isle of Palms City Council approved a drone program that will let dispatchers launch an aircraft from the Public Safety Building when certain 911 calls arrive. Police Chief Kevin Cornett and Fire Chief Craig Oliverius said live video and thermal imaging can help with missing people, swimmers in distress, structure fires and hazardous smoke.

Why It Matters: On a barrier island, getting an aerial view before crews arrive could save time during water rescues and help firefighters find hidden heat without putting more people at risk.

What's Next: Installation and FAA operator training come next; officials want a licensed operator available around the clock.

📚 Main Library Closes for 18 Months

📚 Main Library Closes for 18 Months

The Gist: Charleston County Public Library's Main Library at 68 Calhoun Street will close Aug. 1 for an 18-month renovation funded through the 2014 library referendum. The project will put children's and teen services on the same floor while refreshing public spaces and programming areas.

Why It Matters: The final referendum-funded library renovation temporarily removes a major downtown source of books, computers, programs and staff help, but promises a more accessible branch when it reopens in 2028.

What's Next: Until the temporary branch at 1142 Morrison Drive opens, holds will move to the Keith Summey North Charleston Library and full services remain at John L. Dart Library.

🏭 Mt. Holly Expansion Adds 150 Jobs

🏭 Mt. Holly Expansion Adds 150 Jobs

The Gist: Century Aluminum's Mt. Holly Smelter has reached full capacity after operating at 75%, adding 150 jobs to its existing workforce of 600. The Berkeley County plant now produces 230,000 tonnes of aluminum a year, which company officials say increases domestic primary aluminum output by 10%.

Why It Matters: The expansion restores production lost during past cutbacks and adds industrial jobs at one of the Charleston region's largest manufacturing operations.

🔨 Philip Simmons Sites Clear State Review

🔨 Philip Simmons Sites Clear State Review

The Gist: South Carolina's Board of Review voted unanimously July 24 to nominate 30 and 30 1/2 Blake Street for the National Register of Historic Places. The East Side properties include the 19th-century home, cottage and workshop tied to master blacksmith Philip Simmons, who created about 1,000 ironworks during an eight-decade career.

Why It Matters: National recognition could unlock tax credits and grants needed to repair the properties while preserving a major piece of Charleston's Black cultural and labor history.

What's Next: The National Park Service must make the final decision on adding the two addresses to the register.
