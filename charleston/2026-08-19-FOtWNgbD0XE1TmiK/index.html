🇵🇷 Puerto Rican whole-hog barbecue in West Ashley

At 2366 Ashley River Road in West Ashley, Palmira Barbecue is pitmaster Hector Garate's Puerto Rican take on Carolina whole-hog barbecue. He burns wood and works with local meats, folding his family's flavors into the smoke.

The red-tray room keeps it casual. Beef cheeks, whole hog, barbacoa, and house sausage come with Puerto Rican beans, collards, and hash and rice; Palmira is named for Garate's grandmother, who grew up on a cattle ranch.

🚑 $14M Johns Island Public Safety Hub Advances

The Gist: Charleston County is weighing a $14 million public safety complex on five acres contributed by the Kiawah River development. Plans combine EMS, a law enforcement substation and a satellite dispatch center near a community permitted for 1,000 homes, about 400 of which are occupied.

Why It Matters: Putting three emergency functions on Johns Island could shorten response times in the fast-growing southern part of the county.

What's Next: The county Finance Committee will discuss the plan August 20. If County Council approves it, completion is estimated for 2032.

🚦 $2.26M Traffic Grant Targets Two Busy Corridors

The Gist: Charleston is seeking a $2.26 million federal grant to add adaptive signals along Folly Road and the Crosstown. The project would upgrade all 11 Folly Road signals and bring real-time timing to corridors used by roughly 70,000 and 80,000 vehicles a day, respectively.

Why It Matters: The system could react to beach traffic, crashes and events instead of relying on preset timing plans.

What's Next: Federal officials expect to announce awards between late 2026 and spring 2027. Installation would take six months to a year after funding arrives.

🏖️ Court Voids Folly Beach's 800-Rental Cap

The Gist: A judge invalidated Folly Beach's voter-approved cap of 800 short-term rentals and its registration fee. The ruling said taxation and regulation belong to City Council, not voters, prompting residents to line up at City Hall with new rental applications.

Why It Matters: The decision could reshape housing, tourism and property rights on an island long divided over vacation rentals.

What's Next: A 10-day stay is holding applications without approval or denial while City Council prepares a special session to decide whether to appeal.

Read more here.

☀️ Summerville Lands $15.7M Solar Factory

The Gist: Translucent Solar selected Dorchester County for a 1.2-gigawatt solar panel factory, investing $15,675,000 and creating 167 jobs. The Summerville plant will make high-efficiency photovoltaic modules for utility, commercial, industrial and residential customers.

Why It Matters: The project adds advanced manufacturing jobs and will route about 200 containers of equipment through the Port of Charleston.

What's Next: The company is recruiting now. Equipment arrives in October and November, first production is planned by year-end and full one-shift operations are targeted for early 2027.

Read more here.

🌿 $37M Deal Advances Protection of Captain Sams Spit

The Gist: State lawmakers approved South Carolina's $32 million share of a proposed $37 million settlement for Captain Sams Spit. Kiawah Island would contribute $3.7 million and the Conservancy of the Sea Islands $1.3 million to end nearly two decades of litigation and prohibit development.

Why It Matters: The deal would preserve a major Lowcountry wildlife habitat, protect public access and move state-owned land toward Heritage Trust Preserve status.

What's Next: The settlement still needs court approval and other closing requirements before the land transfers and pending lawsuits are dismissed.
