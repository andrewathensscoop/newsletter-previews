🍗 Fried chicken and oysters in a King Street body shop

At 698 King Street, Leon's Oyster Shop serves fried chicken, oysters, salads, soft serve, and frozen gin and tonics from Leon Ravenel's former auto body shop. Open since 2014, the restaurant kept the functioning garage doors, concrete floors, and some of Ravenel's memorabilia.

The kitchen serves its full menu all day, so char-grilled oysters and fried chicken can share the table at lunch or dinner. Rosé on tap and vanilla soft serve keep the old-garage setup playful.

⚡ Utility Merger Review Gets a Timeline

The Gist: South Carolina's Public Service Commission adopted a schedule Tuesday for reviewing the proposed Dominion Energy and NextEra Energy merger. Petitioner and discovery filings begin in September, with testimony from regulators and other parties due Oct. 28.

Why It Matters: The proceeding gives utility customers and watchdogs a formal chance to scrutinize a deal that would change control of Dominion Energy South Carolina.

What's Next: Petitions to intervene are due Sept. 10. The hearing is Dec. 8, and the commission expects a final order by Jan. 29, 2027.

✈️ Airport Wins $3.5M for Terminal A

The Gist: Charleston International Airport will receive more than $3.5 million in federal funding for its Terminal A expansion and other improvements. The award is part of an $870 million package covering 339 airports in 44 states and two territories.

Why It Matters: The grant moves a major capacity project forward at the region's primary commercial airport.

🏫 DD2 Adds Space for 44 Classrooms

The Gist: Dorchester District Two opened the year with 10 new classrooms at Oakbrook Elementary, 24 at Summerville Elementary and room for 10 more at Ashley Ridge High. Every school also has a secured main-entry vestibule, with additional window film and bullet-resistant doors at select campuses.

Why It Matters: The district is adding space as officials project 10,000 to 15,000 more homes along the East Edisto corridor over the next 15 years.

What's Next: Work is expected to start at Knightsville Elementary, while Newington Elementary's project should finish within the coming year.

Read more here.

🏥 Pediatric Burn Clinic Expands Care

The Gist: Shriners Children's and MUSC Health unveiled a new outpatient pediatric burn clinic in Charleston with five exam rooms, rehabilitation space, physical and occupational therapy, and a child life specialist. It is the fifth American Burn Association-verified pediatric burn center in the Shriners network.

Why It Matters: Children can continue specialized recovery close to home after leaving the hospital, when rehabilitation and follow-up care still matter.

What's Next: The clinic is expected to begin seeing patients this fall.

Read more here.

🚦 IOP Turns to an Adaptive Signal

The Gist: Isle of Palms plans to install an AI-controlled signal at Palm Boulevard and the Isle of Palms Connector. Deputy City Administrator Sean Kuester said the system will detect backups in real time and change the light cycle to clear accumulating traffic.

Why It Matters: The adaptive signal targets everyday island congestion and is intended to move traffic more efficiently ahead of the next busy tourism season.

What's Next: The city expects installation by March 2027 and may consider similar systems elsewhere on the island if it performs as expected.
