Subject: 🍽️ Aiken doubles prepared-food tax to 2%

Preview text: Inside: Columbia schools' 14.997-mill rate, Golisano's new pediatric center, Pace Corners, and Rumours ATL.

  • 🍽️ Aiken doubled its prepared-food tax to 2%, adding an estimated $2.4 million a year

  • 🤝 CSRA nonprofits requested more than $2 million from a grant pool now aiming for $1 million

🥖 Garden Herbs and French Bistro Fare in Hammond's Ferry

At 505 Railroad Avenue in Hammond's Ferry, Manuel's Bread Cafe is chef Manuel Verney-Carron's neighborhood French bistro. Lunch, dinner, weekend breakfast, and Sunday brunch give locals plenty of reasons to stop in.

The garden is part of the kitchen, not just the scenery. Guests can watch chefs bring in herbs and vegetables for the night's plates, and even the table decorations are grown on the farm.

🍽️ Aiken Doubles Hospitality Tax to 2%

The Gist: Aiken City Council voted unanimously Monday to double the city's hospitality tax on prepared food and drinks from 1% to 2%. City Manager Stuart Bedenbaugh said the increase should generate about $2.4 million a year for parks, recreation, tourism facilities and a downtown parking garage tied to the Hotel Aiken renovation.

Why It Matters: Restaurant customers will pay the added penny per dollar, while general retail purchases such as clothing are exempt.

What's Next: The city must amend its already-approved budget before spending the new revenue, and officials say the public will get a chance to weigh in.

Read more here.

🏫 Columbia Schools Cut Tax Rate to 14.997 Mills

The Gist: The Columbia County Board of Education approved a 14.997-mill school tax rate Tuesday, down from 16.234 mills in 2025. It is the board's sixth straight annual reduction and its second consecutive rollback rate.

Why It Matters: At a taxable value of $200,000, WRDW's example puts the school-tax bill about $247 lower than it would be at last year's rate, though assessments and exemptions still shape each bill.

Read more here.

🧠 Pediatric Behavioral Center Opens at Golisano

The Gist: Wellstar Golisano Children's Hospital of Georgia opened the Kisner Foundation and Friends Center for Pediatric Development, Behavioral Health & Wellness in Augusta. The center puts rehabilitation, occupational, physical and speech therapy, developmental-behavioral pediatrics and pediatric neuroscience clinics in one location.

Why It Matters: With 24% of Georgia children reported to have a mental, emotional or behavioral condition, the center gives families one place to reach specialists who were previously scattered across the hospital campus.

What's Next: Wellstar plans to add pediatric psychiatry, though it has not announced a start date.

Read more here.

🤝 $1M CSRA Grant Campaign Opens Final Push

The Gist: Community Foundation for the CSRA launched "One Million Threads. One Shared Mission" to build a $1 million community-grant pool for 2027. Its endowment is expected to supply $700,000 to $750,000 after local nonprofits requested more than $2 million this year, over twice what the foundation could award.

Why It Matters: The grants support nonprofits serving Richmond, Columbia, McDuffie, Burke, Aiken and Edgefield counties, with individual awards capped at $15,000.

What's Next: Applications close Friday, Aug. 14, at 4 p.m.; awards are scheduled for January.

Read more here.

🏘️ Pace Corners Faces Sweep After 12-Plus Code Cases

The Gist: Augusta code enforcement officers went door to door Wednesday at Pace Corners, a 75-unit workforce-housing complex at 1520 Gordon Highway. Records show more than a dozen code cases in two years involving utility shutoffs, sewage and sanitation, while its property manager was operating without a county business license.

Why It Matters: Tenants' leases include utilities, but residents reported days without power, no hot water and months without included WiFi after provider bills went unpaid.
