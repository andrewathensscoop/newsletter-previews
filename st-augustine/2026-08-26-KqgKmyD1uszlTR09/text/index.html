🦐 Minorcan chowder beside the Tolomato River

Aunt Kate's

On the Tolomato River at 612 Euclid Avenue, Aunt Kate's serves local seafood and Minorcan family recipes on the same North Beach site where the Usina family has fed visitors for more than a century. The menu includes Minorcan clam chowder and St. Augustine-style shrimp.

Sit outside beneath the live oaks for sunset across the water, or arrive by boat and tie up at the floating dock. The riverfront deck is the draw, especially around dinner.

🎪 New St. Augustine event fees take effect Oct. 1

🎪 New St. Augustine event fees take effect Oct. 1

The Gist: City commissioners unanimously approved a new events manual for city property and public rights-of-way. Francis Field will cost $300 for up to four hours, $750 for more than four and up to eight hours, and $1,500 for longer rentals, while the special-event permit application rises from $35 to $50.

Why It Matters: The pricing, deposits, blackout dates and clearer shuttle rules will directly affect festivals, races, parades and nonprofits using public spaces.

What's Next: The new fees and permit rules take effect Oct. 1.

⛵ St. Augustine's 64-slip marina nears October opening

⛵ St. Augustine's 64-slip marina nears October opening

The Gist: Sebastian Harbor Marina is scheduled to open in October after its San Sebastian River basin sat unfinished for nearly 20 years. The boutique marina beside the AC Hotel will serve boats up to 55 feet, with dredging, reinforced pilings, floating docks and roughly seven feet of depth at mean low water.

Why It Matters: The marina will add protected dockage and bring visiting boaters within walking distance of downtown businesses and attractions.

What's Next: Construction and final preparations continue; prospective monthly, long-term and transient guests can join an interest list while rates are set.

🏘️ 1,165 acres clear first development hurdle

🏘️ 1,165 acres clear first development hurdle

The Gist: St. Johns County commissioners voted 3-2 to certify the 615-acre Heritage East and 550-acre Heritage West tracts as agricultural enclaves under SB 686. The land near I-95 and State Road 207 could eventually hold nearly 15,000 homes, although no housing plan was approved.

Why It Matters: Certification lets development plans advance through staff review without another commission or public approval, putting major traffic, school and infrastructure questions ahead.

What's Next: A separate 4,462-acre proposal north of Nocatee returns at 9 a.m. Sept. 1; its representatives propose one home per acre.

🏖️ Free shuttle targets Labor Day beach traffic

🏖️ Free shuttle targets Labor Day beach traffic

The Gist: St. Johns County Beach Services will run a free shuttle from Cornerstone Park, 1046 A1A North, to Mickler's Landing from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. Sept. 5-7. Sheriff's deputies, St. Augustine Beach police and Marine Rescue will patrol, with lifeguards stationed from 10 a.m. to 6 p.m.

Why It Matters: The service gives beachgoers another way around limited parking and congestion during one of the year's busiest weekends while adding visible safety coverage.

What's Next: On-beach driving runs from 8 a.m. to 7:30 p.m.; conditions can change, so check SJC Connect or the county's beach updates before leaving.

🏠 92 affordable apartments now leasing in West Augustine

🏠 92 affordable apartments now leasing in West Augustine

The Gist: The Village of New Augustine is complete at 280 N. Volusia St. and is actively leasing 92 one-, two- and three-bedroom apartments. Every unit is reserved for households earning up to 60% of the area median income, with current rents ranging from about $634 to $1,599 based on apartment and household size and income.

Why It Matters: St. Johns County put more than $1.6 million into the $26.5 million development, adding income-restricted rentals as the county's population grows.

What's Next: The community is actively leasing one-, two- and three-bedroom apartments.
