🫓 Honduran Baleadas on West University Avenue

Tela

At 1702 W. University Avenue, Tela serves Honduran-inspired bowls, burritos, and baleadas from two childhood friends who first met in kindergarten in Honduras and later reconnected in the United States.

The build-your-own menu layers red beans, plantains, pickled onions, cotija, and Tela sauce with chicken, chorizo, steak, or vegetables. It is a fast-casual taste of the coastal city that gave the restaurant its name and the baleada its home.

🏛️ County unanimously backs immigration-enforcement resolution

🏛️ County unanimously backs immigration-enforcement resolution

The Gist: Alachua County commissioners voted unanimously Aug. 11 for a resolution addressing immigration-related enforcement. It says the county will follow Florida law while expecting enforcement to be nondiscriminatory and nondestructive, and it stops short of naming ICE or creating a sanctuary policy.

Why It Matters: County Attorney Sylvia Torres said the state attorney general could challenge the action, while officials also acknowledged possible risks to federal grants, including HUD funding.

💡 GRU keeps investment-grade ratings before $150 million bond sale

💡 GRU keeps investment-grade ratings before $150 million bond sale

The Gist: Fitch, Moody's and S&P affirmed stable, investment-grade ratings for Gainesville Regional Utilities before its planned $150 million bond issue. The ratings remain A+ from Fitch, Aa3 from Moody's and A from S&P, with another $150 million bond planned for 2028.

Why It Matters: The new debt will help fund work including Main Street Water Reclamation Facility upgrades, while GRU's high debt load and governance dispute remain risks cited by the agencies.

What's Next: GRU plans to sell the bonds later in August. Water rates rise 2% and wastewater rates 1.75% in October.

🌳 Gainesville collected $14.1 million in tree-removal fees

🌳 Gainesville collected $14.1 million in tree-removal fees

The Gist: Gainesville collected $10.3 million from projects that removed regulated trees between fiscal 2019 and 2024, then another $3.8 million the next year. The city spent $110,800 on tree planting during the first five years and allocated $4 million for land purchases in 2026.

Why It Matters: With limited room for new trees, the city is shifting toward buying existing woods, including land near Main Street and 39th Avenue and Four Creeks Preserve.

What's Next: UF researchers plan to present an updated inventory of more than 25,000 public trees to city commissioners this fall.

🚸 School-zone cameras now carry $100 speeding fines

🚸 School-zone cameras now carry $100 speeding fines

The Gist: Alachua County's school-zone cameras now mail $100 fines to drivers going at least 11 mph over the limit while a zone is active, or at least 15 mph over while school is in session. The administrative fine initially carries no license points or insurance report.

Why It Matters: The cameras capture a three-second video and four photos only after detecting a violation, extending enforcement when deputies cannot cover every school zone.

What's Next: Recipients have 30 days to pay and a 15-day grace period before the fine can become a traffic citation with higher penalties and possible points.

⚖️ Two public defenders vie for County Judge Seat 5

⚖️ Two public defenders vie for County Judge Seat 5

The Gist: Eric Atria and AuBroncee Martin, each a public defender for more than 20 years, are competing for Alachua County Judge Seat 5 in the Aug. 18 election. County judges handle criminal misdemeanors and civil disputes involving up to $50,000.

Why It Matters: The winner will oversee everyday local cases involving traffic, landlords and tenants, small claims and misdemeanors in the county's most community-facing trial court.

What's Next: Early voting runs through Aug. 15 from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m. Election Day polls are open from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Aug. 18.
