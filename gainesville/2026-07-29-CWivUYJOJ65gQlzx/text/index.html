The Swamp Restaurant

At 1104 SW 2nd Avenue, The Swamp Restaurant is the revived version of a Gainesville restaurant, bar, and event venue that first opened near UF in 1994. The original closed in 2020, and The Swamp returned nearby in August 2022.

The new home brings back the front lawn, white picket fence, live music, and historic-house look that generations of Gators remember. It is still built for game-day crowds and long afternoons outside.

🏌️ Gainesville opens Ironwood bidding after $1.1 million offer

🏌️ Gainesville opens Ironwood bidding after $1.1 million offer

The Gist: Gainesville opened bidding to buy or lease Ironwood Golf Course after receiving HangTime Golf's $1.1 million purchase offer and $2.4 million first-year investment plan. The city requires the property to remain a public golf course, and a local group called The Track is preparing another proposal.

Why It Matters: Ironwood logged 35,000 rounds but ran a $322,683 deficit in fiscal 2025, so the choice could shift costs off city books while determining the future of one of Alachua County's two public courses.

What's Next: Bids are due by 3 p.m. Aug. 22, and city staff expect to begin evaluations Sept. 7.

🏗️ Alachua advances 182-acre Farmlands rezoning 3-2

🏗️ Alachua advances 182-acre Farmlands rezoning 3-2

The Gist: After a 12-hour hearing, the Alachua City Commission voted 3-2 to advance rezoning for 182 acres of Farmlands Commercial along U.S. 441. The ordinance includes four development-code exceptions and requires an engineering and environmental study of stormwater impacts on a contamination plume at the nearby closed Northwest Landfill.

Why It Matters: The vote moves a major residential and commercial project that references a Walmart closer to approval while traffic, groundwater, sinkhole and contamination concerns remain.

What's Next: The final rezoning vote is scheduled for Aug. 24. If approved, the applicant will have one year to submit a plat or site plan.

🏘️ Gainesville backs 96 affordable apartments with $460,000

🏘️ Gainesville backs 96 affordable apartments with $460,000

The Gist: City commissioners unanimously approved a $460,000 ConnectFree loan for Hope at Debra Heights, a proposed $31 million development with 96 apartments in four three-story buildings along Northeast Second Street. The one-, two- and three-bedroom units would serve residents earning up to 70% of the Gainesville area median income.

Why It Matters: The local contribution strengthens the project's bid for state low-income housing tax credits and could add family-sized rental homes in northeast Gainesville.

What's Next: The state application is due Aug. 11, with a decision expected early next year. If the credits are denied, the $460,000 stays in the ConnectFree program.

🏛️ High Springs votes 4-1 to move toward firing city manager

🏛️ High Springs votes 4-1 to move toward firing city manager

The Gist: The High Springs City Commission voted 4-1 to place City Manager Jeremy Marshall on immediate administrative leave with the intent to terminate him. Commissioners and residents pointed to a missed $900,000 bill, a late audit that prompted the state to withhold funds and other financial oversight concerns.

Why It Matters: An interim team now inherits the late audit and a proposed 7.24-mill ceiling that could raise nearly $500,000 more in property taxes.

What's Next: The commission is scheduled to vote on a termination resolution Aug. 13. Jennifer Applebee and Alan Alligood are sharing interim city-manager duties.

🏫 Waldo school opens Aug. 10 with about 60 students

🏫 Waldo school opens Aug. 10 with about 60 students

The Gist: Grace Christian Prep School will open a private K-12 campus in the Noe Wing of Waldo City Hall for the 2026-27 school year, with about 60 students already enrolled. Waldo had no school after Constellation Charter School closed last year over safety violations.

Why It Matters: The opening gives families in the city of roughly 900 residents a local option again instead of requiring every student to commute elsewhere in Alachua County.

What's Next: Classes begin Aug. 10. Registration remains open, and tuition scholarships are available through Step Up for Students.
