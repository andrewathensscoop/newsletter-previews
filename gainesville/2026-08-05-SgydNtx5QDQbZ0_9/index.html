🎶 Southern and Haitian plates on NW 4th Street

In Gainesville's historic Pleasant Street neighborhood at 732 NW 4th Street, The Dunbar pairs Southern cooking with the chef's Haitian roots, Afro-Caribbean touches, and a menu that changes with the seasons.

An intimate dining room, courtyard, and patio frame a restaurant built around Gainesville's artistic and musical legacy. Reservations are recommended inside, while the outdoor spaces remain open for walk-ins when weather allows.

🏗️ Gainesville breaks ground on $22.2 million safety center

The Gist: Gainesville broke ground July 31 on a $22.2 million Southwest Public Safety Center funded by the countywide infrastructure surtax. The site will house Fire Station 9, the city's first police precinct away from headquarters, a storm-hardened operations center and a 1,100-square-foot community room.

Why It Matters: Southwest Gainesville will gain permanent fire quarters, a nearby police base and its first public community space, replacing Station 9's temporary trailer setup.

What's Next: The city plans a 2027 ribbon cutting and will plant 124 native trees after removing 521 regulated trees from the 7.5-acre site.

💊 $56.1 million Alachua drug plant targets 400 jobs

The Gist: Strive Compounding Pharmacy is setting up operations in Alachua's 270,000-square-foot former Resilience plant, valued with its equipment at $56.1 million. The facility has 30 full-time employees and plans to grow to about 400 positions in engineering, microbiology, chemistry and pharmaceutical sciences.

Why It Matters: The project returns specialized manufacturing to a site left by Resilience's July 2025 closure, which caused more than 100 layoffs, and could supply shortage drugs to nearby hospitals.

What's Next: Operations will roll out in three phases beginning in October, with the full buildout scheduled for September 2027.

Read more here.

🏫 Alachua County schools advance $610.8 million budget

The Gist: The School Board of Alachua County unanimously approved a tentative $610,840,438 budget for 2026-27, up $6.7 million from last year. The proposed 6.327-mill rate includes a state-required local effort increase, while $179.9 million of the general fund is budgeted for instruction.

Why It Matters: The plan directs the county's school spending on teachers, transportation, utilities and construction while setting the property-tax rate needed to receive state funding.

What's Next: The final budget and millage hearing is scheduled for 5:01 p.m. Sept. 15.

Read more here.

🚒 High Springs Fire earns record Class 3 rating

The Gist: High Springs Fire Department will move from ISO Class 4 to Class 3 on Nov. 1, its best Public Protection Classification and first improvement since 2013. The five-year review scores emergency communications, staffing, equipment, training, water supply and prevention work.

Why It Matters: The stronger rating recognizes improved fire readiness and may lower some homeowners' insurance premiums, though it does not change the city's fire assessment fee.

What's Next: Homeowners can contact their insurers after the Nov. 1 effective date to ask whether the new rating changes their premiums.

Read more here.

🏘️ First tenant opens at Newberry's 128-acre Highland Park

The Gist: Advance Auto Parts opened July 31 at 24093 W. Newberry Road as the first tenant in Highland Park, two years after Newberry broke ground on the 128-acre development. Plans call for 150,000 square feet of commercial space and 350 homes, with Wawa, Chipotle, McDonald's, Heartland Dental and a self-storage facility approved or under review.

Why It Matters: The opening starts the buildout of a major mixed-use project expected to add shops and housing, and Newberry already installed its second traffic light for Highland Park traffic.

What's Next: Advance Auto Parts will hold its grand opening Aug. 15.
